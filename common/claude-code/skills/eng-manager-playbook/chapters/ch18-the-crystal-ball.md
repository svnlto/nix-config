# Chapter 18: The Crystal Ball

## Core Idea

A compelling ten-year vision paired with a concrete one-year plan and a
tracked skills backlog is the engine of career happiness — and a manager
should build one for themselves first, then coach each report through
the same exercise.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Vision-and-Plan career exercise**: a self-guided exercise moving
  from past reflection to a long-term vision to a short-term plan to a
  working skills backlog.
  - When to use: for your own career development, and repeated with each
    direct report as a coaching series.
  - How: (1) plot the last ten years on a timeline; (2) write a
    ten-year vision; (3) write a plan for five/three/two/one years out;
    (4) rate yourself on competencies; (5) derive a skills backlog and
    revisit it regularly.

- **Ten-year timeline reflection**: draw a line for the last ten years
  and plot significant life and career events on it.
  - When to use: as the opening step, to prove to yourself how much can
    change in a decade and unlock bigger ambition.
  - How: include education, moves, and personal milestones, not just
    jobs; then attribute each event to Training, Luck, People,
    Forethought, and Hindsight in a table and note which mattered most.

- **Ten-year vision statement**: a first-person, present-tense
  description of your life a decade out.
  - When to use: after the timeline, to define a magnetic long-term
    target most people either underdream or leave too vague.
  - How: write "I will…" statements covering role, location, lifestyle,
    and the people around you; cover current rewarding achievements,
    what excites you now, whether you want to manage, and your
    qualifications; make it almost touchable.

- **Plan statement (five/three/two/one-year backcast)**: work backward
  from the vision to concrete near-term actions.
  - When to use: once the vision exists, to answer "what must I do this
    year to move toward it?"
  - How: for each horizon jot the skills, opportunities, training, and
    networking needed; specify concrete, provable actions and a clear
    definition of success for the year.

- **Competency rating + skills backlog**: rate yourself against your
  career-ladder competencies, then turn plan actions into tracked
  backlog items.
  - When to use: to make the plan measurable and to keep progress
    visible over the year.
  - How: rate each competency (e.g. Technical knowledge L3, Mentorship
    L2); map plan actions to the competencies they advance; record them
    in a tool like Trello; review quarterly with your manager, pruning
    done items and adding new ones.

## Key Concepts

- **Ikigai**: Okinawan term for "what you live for" — a compelling
  future goal that propels you forward; distinct from wealth or comfort.
- **Purpose**: not economic status or well-being, but having a life
  worth living; a shared trait of the longest-lived communities.
- **Tours of duty**: framing each role as a defined step that reaches
  the next career stage without pretending employment is permanent.
- **Vision vs. plan**: the long-term vision you always move toward
  versus the current plan defined roughly one year out.
- **Skills backlog**: a living, regularly updated list of concrete
  skill-building actions mapped to competencies.
- **Career fluidity**: the fact that your vision of the future will keep
  changing, so a plan is a direction, not a fixed contract.

## Mental Models

- Think of the future as looking out the window of a moving train — it
  manifests through luck, opportunity, willpower, and acceptance, not
  fixed prediction.
- Use the ten-year backward look to calibrate the ten-year forward
  look: people underestimate how much happens in a decade.
- Aim for the vision but enjoy the journey — "having something to aim
  for will make you happy if you mindfully enjoy the journey rather than
  obsessing over the destination."
- When coaching, listen beyond the words: "I want to work in Product"
  may really mean "I want to be chief product officer at my own
  startup."

## Anti-patterns

- **Not dreaming big enough**: a vision that can't see past the next
  promotion or pay raise has no magnetic pull.
- **A vague vision**: without vivid, specific detail it fails to propel
  you toward it.
- **Treating the ten-year plan as inflexible**: rigidly sticking to it
  makes you unhappy because life always changes.
- **A vision that's just a laundry list**: a to-do list is not a vision;
  it should resonate and feel authentic.
- **Skipping outside input**: not testing the vision and competency
  self-ratings against partners, friends, and peers hides blind spots.

## Worked Example

The chapter walks through J. Bloggs' completed exercise. The vision:
"In ten years, I want to be the CTO of a technology company that I have
co-founded… favor purpose over profit… a remote company… living with my
family close to nature in a bright, spacious house." It grounds the
dream in the present — first-time manager, helped junior staff beat
impostor syndrome, excited about shipping a big product — and dismisses
the college dropout worry as irrelevant given a track record of
progress through focus and confidence.

The plan backcasts that vision into this year: excel in the current
manager role to learn CTO fundamentals; specifically fix a known
weakness (delegating effectively) to raise team output; build confidence
in challenging communications by giving praise and critique whenever
warranted; and grow a local network via social media, online groups, and
meetups. Success is defined concretely: a positive performance review
plus supportive peer feedback. Competencies are self-rated (Technical
knowledge L3, Mentorship L2, Influence L2, Consensus building L3,
Communication L2, Output L2), and each plan action is mapped to the
competency it advances and dropped into a Trello skills backlog reviewed
quarterly with the manager.

The reader then runs the same five-step coaching series with staff:
Step 1 ten-years timeline homework; Step 2 ten-years coaching session
(coach role, provocative questions, summarize back with "So what I
hear is… Is that a fair summary?"); Step 3 review their vision for
authenticity; Step 4 competency scoring plus peer feedback and plan
homework; Step 5 one-year coaching session that reconciles self-vs-peer
rankings with radical candor and turns the plan into measurable backlog
items — revisiting the backlog every three months and refining every
six.

## Key Takeaways

1. Build your own vision and plan before coaching anyone else — you
   can't guide the exercise you haven't done.
2. Anchor ambition by first plotting ten years of past events; the
   past proves how much a decade can hold.
3. Write the vision in vivid first-person present tense so it has
   magnetic pull, then backcast it into a one-year plan of provable
   actions.
4. Make the plan measurable by mapping actions to career-ladder
   competencies and tracking them in a living skills backlog.
5. Seek external input on both vision and competency ratings; reconcile
   self-assessment gaps with peers using radical candor.
6. Revisit the skills backlog quarterly and refine the plan every six
   months; treat the vision as a direction, not a fixed destination.
7. Be more than a manager to your reports — be their career coach.
