# Glossary

Key terms from *Become an Effective Software Engineering Manager* by
James Stanier. Chapter references in the last column.

| Term | Definition | Ch |
|------|------------|----|
| **10% R-mode time** | Roughly a tenth of each week blocked to do nothing but let thoughts emerge, away from the desk. | 13 |
| **Abdication** | Handing over both responsibility and accountability (fire-and-forget), which is not delegation. | 3 |
| **Action list** | Prioritized output of the snapshot, sorted into items to raise with the team versus the manager. | 1 |
| **Alignment** | Observations you, your team, and your manager all share -- settled common ground, positive or negative. | 1 |
| **Andon Cord** | A metaphorical stop-cord (from Toyota via Mike Rother's Toyota Kata) either party can pull to halt an uncomfortable topic and ask why. | 4 |
| **Archive never delete** | Email is a transactional record; archive everything read or actioned, search later, delete nothing. | 2 |
| **As above, so below** | You both receive scrutiny from reports and feel it toward those above; align misgivings with your manager from empathy, not resentment. | 10 |
| **Attrition / turnover** | Natural loss of staff — US tech runs ~13.2% yearly, the highest of any sector, so replacement is constant. | 8 |
| **Basic needs** | The bottom two Maslow layers (physiological, safety); money largely lives here, so more past sufficiency doesn't raise happiness. | 5 |
| **Bazaar browser** | A person who thrives on variety, change, chaos, prototyping, and sampling as many new things as possible. | 5 |
| **Being a role model** | Publicly acting with the values and behaviors you want others to adopt. | 9 |
| **Bottom-up improvement** | Starting a practice in your team and letting it gain momentum across the department without a mandate. | 14 |
| **Burn-out** | WHO 2019 syndrome from chronic unmanaged workplace stress: energy depletion, job cynicism, reduced efficacy. | 13 |
| **Cadence** | Every report, every week, same time and place, in a private room, signaling reliable presence. | 4 |
| **Calendar discipline** | The calendar is only for organizing time -- meetings, busy blocks, focus blocks -- never a to-do list. | 2 |
| **Campsite rule** | Leave each area of code you touch better than you found it. | 11 |
| **Capture principle** | Keep as little in your head as possible; offload everything into trusted tools. | 2 |
| **Career fluidity** | The fact that your vision of the future keeps changing, so a plan is a direction rather than a fixed contract. | 18 |
| **Career-level proximal development** | Using skill trees to push a person's development frontier toward ambitious long-term goals. | 5 |
| **Cathedral constructor** | A person who thrives on focus, stability, purism, deep mastery, and being the company expert on one thing. | 5 |
| **Closed box** | Information whose existence is public but whose details are withheld — the hardest and most-mishandled category. | 12 |
| **Coaching** | A framework of conversational tools that helps anyone improve performance and solve their own problems, even in domains you don't know. | 9 |
| **Code vicinity** | Avoiding multiple engineers editing the same code to prevent merge conflicts. | 11 |
| **Colorless diversity** | The phenomenon where efforts to fix imbalance focus on white women and under-invest in people of color. | 16 |
| **Communication as routing** | Delivery schemes mapped to work: unicast (DM), broadcast (all-hands), multicast (channel/guild), anycast (asking whoever walks past). | 14 |
| **Communication channels** | n(n-1)/2 potential channels in a group; a 400-person company has 79,800, which is why growth adds overhead. | 11 |
| **Compass not GPS** | A framework gives a general direction to find your own way, not a fixed route to follow. | 15 |
| **Competency** | A skill dimension (e.g. influence, mentorship) described differently at each level of the grid. | 15 |
| **Confidentiality default** | An explicit agreement on whether meeting content can be relayed to colleagues or upward to your boss. | 4 |
| **Conway's Law** | Organizations are constrained to design systems that copy their own communication structures. | 14 |
| **Counteroffer** | A formulated change in compensation, team, hours, or work type, delivered in person and by email with days to consider. | 8 |
| **Cross-functional team** | Owns a feature's whole lifecycle but without communication becomes just another kind of silo. | 14 |
| **Cultural issues** | Organizational problems (harassment, pay gaps, exclusion) that mostly affect inclusion and are underweighted. | 16 |
| **Culture** | The effect your company, team, and management style have on others; the psychologically safe environment, not perks. | 16 |
| **Culture add (vs culture fit)** | You cannot 'fit' a culture; good hires contribute to and extend it, so hire for what a person adds, not sameness. | 7 |
| **Decoupling pay from performance** | Delivering salary news at a separate time so money worry doesn't drown out the performance conversation. | 6 |
| **Development, not failures** | Framing all critique as development even for struggling staff; the section is named for growth, not blame. | 6 |
| **Diversity** | The makeup of your workforce; who is actually in it. | 16 |
| **Dunning-Kruger effect** | A 1999-documented cognitive bias of illusory superiority in people of lower ability who cannot see their own poor decisions. | 10 |
| **Email as primary inbox** | Route all other tools (chat, tickets) to email you so mastering email keeps you on top of everything. | 2 |
| **Facilitator, not dictator** | In the review you serve the report and guide the conversation rather than hand down a verdict. | 6 |
| **Fair PIP** | A plan with achievable goals genuinely aimed at recovery — unachievable PIPs are unethical and potentially unlawful. | 8 |
| **False beliefs** | Manager and team agree on something your outside experience tells you is wrong. | 1 |
| **False productivity** | The reactive dopamine loop of clearing notifications and inboxes that feels like work but crowds out real impact. | 13 |
| **Five stages of receiving bad news** | Ignore, deny, blame others, assume responsibility, find a solution; sharing writing early lets people reach stage 5 by the meeting. | 6 |
| **Flow** | Csikszentmihalyi's state of fully immersed, enjoyable concentration; the zone makers rely on. | 1 |
| **Formal warning** | The escalation after a PIP breach — evidenced, HR-approved, delivered in a meeting as the last chance. | 8 |
| **Gatekeeping** | Filtering information for further distribution, deciding what, when, and how to broadcast it. | 12 |
| **Gender-coded language** | Masculine- or feminine-themed wording that subconsciously excludes applicants, checkable with a gender-decoder tool. | 7 |
| **Good leaver** | Someone who exits amicably, gets a reference, hands over cleanly, and is welcomed back if the new role fails. | 8 |
| **Holacracy / Teal** | Alternative self-organizing governance structures (circles, self-management) used by some startups instead of a CEO-down tree. | 17 |
| **Hustle culture** | The industry's 10x, grind-at-all-costs norm that normalizes burnout and workaholism. | 16 |
| **Ikigai** | Okinawan term for 'what you live for' — a compelling future goal that propels you forward, distinct from wealth or comfort. | 18 |
| **Impact** | The observable measure of IC seniority: code quality, independent contribution, collaboration, mentorship, influence, innovation, and effect on the bottom line. | 15 |
| **Implicit bias** | Deeply ingrained prejudice we act on without intending to. | 16 |
| **Impostor syndrome** | The internal sense of being a fraud despite being qualified -- normal for new managers. | 1, 10 |
| **Inbox zero** | An aspirational guide to keep the inbox empty, useful as a guide but not a doctrine worth sacrificing real work for. | 2 |
| **Inclusion** | How you build an environment where everyone is psychologically safe and able to contribute; the seat at the table, not just the invite. | 16 |
| **Individual contributor (IC)** | Someone whose currency is technical expertise and influence and who line-manages nobody. | 15 |
| **Information gathering** | The informal, anywhere-anytime feeding of your knowledge base that decisions are later built on. | 2 |
| **Interdependent team** | A cross-functional unit (engineers, designers, QA, agile practitioner, PM, EM) whose members depend on each other, so balance beats raw individual talent. | 7 |
| **Internal vs external goals** | Internal goals judge your effort in the moment; external goals judge results you do not command and set you up to fail. | 13 |
| **Keep the thought bubble over their head** | Prompting the report's own reflections rather than supplying your conclusions. | 6 |
| **Known knowns / unknowns** | Rumsfeld's distillation of how seniority increases both access to sensitive information and the care required to handle it. | 12 |
| **Layoff / redundancy** | A special involuntary case where the employee did nothing wrong; a business decision, a surprise, delivered on a company script. | 8 |
| **Leading questions** | Open prompts that stimulate discussion whether or not you know the answer. | 4 |
| **Leave nobody behind** | The manager's duty to keep everyone seeing the same version of the same information. | 12 |
| **Level** | A stage in a track, written 'L' plus a number, lined up across both ladders by experience. | 15 |
| **Leverage** | A small investment of time producing disproportionately large output; weekly one-to-ones are a prime place to find it. | 4 |
| **Maker's schedule vs manager's schedule** | Paul Graham's distinction that makers need half-day blocks while managers work in hourly slots. | 1 |
| **Manager number one** | The first person in engineering focused on management of people, process, hiring, communication, and delivery. | 17 |
| **Manager span of control** | Rule of thumb of at most ~7 direct reports before one-to-one time becomes prohibitive. | 15 |
| **Measure twice, cut once** | Think before broadcasting because a manager's word carries org-chart weight and cannot be uncut. | 3 |
| **Mentoring** | A long-lived, mostly directive teacher/student relationship sharing experience and skills to help someone progress. | 9 |
| **Mentorship matrix** | A shared spreadsheet (name, title, team, contact, skills sought, skills offered) that kick-starts a department-wide mentoring culture. | 9 |
| **Micromanaging** | Retaining accountability over process rather than outputs; you should own outputs, not method. | 3 |
| **Negative visualization** | A Stoic practice of imagining loss to both prepare for it and appreciate what you have now. | 13 |
| **No meetings please block** | A recurring multi-hour calendar entry that protects uninterrupted deep-work time. | 2 |
| **No-surprises principle** | Pertinent information is written, exchanged, and digested before the meeting; a review is a checkpoint, not a big reveal. | 6 |
| **Nudging** | Influencing a decision you don't own by contributing your viewpoint, remembering your word carries authority. | 2, 9 |
| **Output equation** | Your output equals your team's output plus the output of others you influence. | 3 |
| **Output of a manager** | The output of your team plus the output of others you influence — the metric hiring should maximize. | 7 |
| **Pipeline** | People moving through education into industry; a real but overweighted contributor to the diversity problem. | 16 |
| **Poor communication downward** | You and your manager see something the team is unaware of or disagrees with. | 1 |
| **Poor communication upward** | You and your team see something your manager is unaware of. | 1 |
| **Presenteeism** | Sitting at your desk waiting for others to leave, often doing little productive. | 16 |
| **Problem-solution fit / MVP** | Proving a buildable product solves a business problem, tested via a minimum viable product. | 17 |
| **Product-market fit** | The point where a product reliably meets real market demand; most failed startups never reach it. | 17 |
| **Productivity per head** | The accurate framing of perceived slowdown as a natural side effect of growth, not lazy individuals. | 11 |
| **Pull, don't push** | Proactively drive the relationship with your manager rather than waiting to be directed. | 3 |
| **Purpose** | Not economic status or well-being but having a life worth living; a shared trait of the longest-lived communities. | 18 |
| **RACI** | Responsible/accountable/consulted/informed matrix, often overkill and forgotten, which is why DRIs exist. | 14 |
| **Reframing** | Recasting a raw, emotionally charged message into an honest but constructive one for the team — never hiding facts, only recasting them. | 10 |
| **Rolling agenda** | A shared document both parties add to over the week so there is always material to discuss. | 4 |
| **Root-cause analysis** | Uncovering the real reason for a problem so it does not recur, rather than patching the symptom. | 14 |
| **Screening call** | A 15-30 minute video call to form an initial connection and resolve resume questions like relocation, visa, notice, and salary expectations. | 7 |
| **Scrutiny and judgment** | Rising seniority turns you from contributor into the conceptual representation of your team, inviting nitpicking from every direction. | 10 |
| **Self-actualization** | Top of Maslow's pyramid: realizing full potential through mastery and goals; the need most under a manager's influence. | 5 |
| **Silence is golden** | Leaving pauses unfilled lets reports surface the issues that matter most to them. | 4 |
| **Six-month cadence** | Review twice a year (year-end plus mid-year); yearly is too infrequent, quarterly too often. | 6 |
| **Skill-set team** | Concentrates one discipline's expertise but silos into a physical manifestation of the software stack. | 14 |
| **Skills backlog** | A living, regularly updated list of concrete skill-building actions mapped to competencies. | 18 |
| **Snapshot** | The recorded state of the team's reality when you join, assembled from three separate views. | 1 |
| **Stretch goals** | Milestone planning that assigns Musts plus a few Shoulds to a first release, giving buffers to drop under delay. | 11 |
| **Suitability trumps efficiency** | Choose the delivery best for the recipient, not the one that saves you work. | 3 |
| **Take-home test** | An optional, short (couple-hours), clearly-scoped exercise mirroring real work, useful for nervous candidates and graduates. | 7 |
| **Task parallelism** | How much scope is sequential versus concurrent, determining whether adding people can help. | 11 |
| **Task-level proximal development** | Delegating a stretch task and pairing a junior with a senior so both grow, one in skill and one in mentoring. | 5 |
| **The emotion scale** | How a team feels toward you: kindness/understanding -> concern/worry -> resentment -> mutiny; further down means more work to recover. | 10 |
| **The energy bar** | A manager's finite store of authority, depleted by controversial overrides and unpopular orders, replenished by moving congruently with the team. | 12 |
| **The output equation** | Your output = the output of your team + the output of others that you influence. | 9 |
| **The Peter Principle** | Laurence J. Peter's observation that people are promoted on past success until they reach a level where they are no longer competent. | 15 |
| **The snow melts at the periphery** | Staff on the business edges (sales, customer success) see market reality before the core does, so connecting with them speeds reaction. | 9 |
| **To-do list as single home** | If it isn't on your to-do list, you don't do it until it is. | 2 |
| **Too much talent effect** | In highly interdependent teams, talent boosts performance only to a point, after which coordination suffers and output falls. | 7 |
| **Tours of duty** | Framing a role as a defined, mutually beneficial stint rather than lifetime employment. | 17, 18 |
| **Tranquility** | The Stoic term for peace of mind, protected by not staking your emotions on outcomes you cannot control. | 13 |
| **Trends toward transparency** | The default should be to share with care, not keep secret; silence makes people assume the worst. | 12 |
| **Unicorn** | A startup valued at over $1 billion. | 17 |
| **Verbal communication** | Spoken and written combined; the author splits them because each has distinct considerations. | 3 |
| **Vesting / stock options** | Equity granted over time as compensation, often in lieu of high base salary, that may never convert to real money. | 17 |
| **Vision vs. plan** | The long-term vision you always move toward versus the current plan defined roughly one year out. | 18 |
| **Warning signs** | Escalating cues (hallway probing, sudden senior interest, external hype, growing ticket queue) that the Eye has turned onto your team. | 11 |
| **Weekly written summary** | A thirty-minute reflective note to your manager that opens your world and acts as managerial rubber ducking. | 3 |
| **Workplace politics** | How people in groups make decisions; a neutral force with positive and negative uses, not inherently backstabbing. | 12 |
| **Zinger** | A departure that blindsides you but was preventable in retrospect, caused by simmering issues never surfaced. | 8 |
| **Zone of proximal development** | The learning sweet spot between what a person can do alone and what they cannot yet do at all. | 5, 10 |
